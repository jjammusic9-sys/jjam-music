04 / About

Music made by people.

J JAM is an independent music collective working from Iraq and across the Arab region. We create, produce, release and share music while building practical ways for artists, communities and creative projects to use music in their own work.

The project brings together original releases, collaborations, experiments and an open music library. At its centre is a simple idea: music can be both an artistic practice and a resource that helps other people make things.

01 / Why J JAM

Build the space around the people.

A space for independent work

J JAM grows from the work of musicians, producers, artists and collaborators connected to Iraq and the wider Arab region. We are interested in work that does not always fit neatly into commercial music structures: experiments, unfinished ideas, private jams, collaborations and sounds developed outside the mainstream.

Access matters

Many creative projects need music but do not have the budget, networks or time to commission a soundtrack from scratch. Our library is one practical response to that gap. We make selected original tracks available under clear terms so independent media, filmmakers, artists, educators, youth projects and civil society initiatives can find music they can actually use.

Culture is a shared resource

We see contemporary music as part of the cultural life of the region: something to make, document, circulate and build on. Sharing resources can create connections between artists and organisations, support new projects and help creative work move between communities and contexts.

02 / How we work

Make. Collaborate. Share.

Music

We produce and release original music through J JAM, including individual work, collective productions, collaborations and experimental projects. Some works begin as personal tracks; others grow through conversations, shared sessions and remote collaboration.

Collaboration

J JAM is open to working with musicians, filmmakers, visual artists, cultural organisations, independent media and other creative initiatives. Collaboration can mean producing a track, developing music for a project, building a soundtrack, documenting a musical process or creating something new together.

Submissions

Artists can send us tracks, demos or ideas through the Submit page. A submission is not an automatic publishing request: we listen, select projects that fit the collective and, when there is a shared interest, develop them together as a J JAM production or collaboration.

03 / Open music library

Music people can use.

For creative work

The J JAM Library is a growing collection of original tracks made to support other forms of creative work — from short films and documentaries to podcasts, video, visual art, education, independent journalism, youth productions and community projects.

Clear and practical

Each library track comes with its own usage information. Our current library focuses on free non-profit use, with the intention of making the terms understandable rather than creating another barrier for people working with limited resources.

More than background music

We want the library to connect the people who make music with the people who need it. When a track travels into a film, podcast, exhibition, campaign, classroom or community project, the music becomes part of another story while the artist behind it remains visible.

04 / People & technology

Human-made, visible, credited.

No generative AI

J JAM does not use generative AI to make its music. The sounds in our releases and library come from people: their ideas, performances, instruments, production, editing, recording and collaboration.

People stay visible

Credits matter to us. We want artists and collaborators to be identifiable, and we want the context around a piece of music to remain available when that work moves into another project.

A growing network

J JAM is not built around a fixed roster. The collective can grow through new artists, producers, collaborators and partner organisations, while keeping the focus on meaningful work rather than simply increasing the number of releases.

05 / Work with J JAM

Let’s build something useful.

For organisations & initiatives

We are interested in partnerships with cultural organisations, civil society groups, independent media, educational projects, festivals and creative initiatives that want to use music as part of their work or develop something together.

For artists & collaborators

If you are a musician, producer, filmmaker, visual artist or another creative practitioner and see a way we could work together, get in touch. We are especially interested in projects that create opportunities for people, exchange knowledge or make creative resources more accessible.

Support & partnerships

J JAM is still growing. Support can help us produce new music, pay artists and collaborators, maintain the library, develop creative projects and build the infrastructure needed to keep the work accessible.
